Exocad vs 3Shape for dental labs

Both are excellent. The honest answer is that they suit different labs — and for many, the smartest move is not to buy either. Here's how they really differ on license, ecosystem and cost, and how to decide.

By Alejandro Carvajal · Designs daily in both 7 min read Updated Nov 2026

Ask ten technicians which CAD is better and you'll get ten answers, usually shaped by whichever one they learned first. The truth is less tribal: Exocad and 3Shape are both industry-standard, and both produce excellent restorations. The differences that actually matter to a lab are the business model and how open the ecosystem is — not the quality ceiling.

Exocad — where it wins

Exocad's DentalCAD is built around ownership and openness. You pay once for a perpetual license and choose exactly the modules you need (Implant, Smile Creator, Bite Splint, Model Creator, and more).

  • Perpetual license — no recurring fee to keep designing; updates are optional.
  • Scanner-agnostic — it doesn't care which scanner produced the STL, so it fits a mixed hardware shop.
  • Modular cost — start small and add modules as your case mix grows.
  • Huge tooth and implant libraries and a wide third-party integration network.

3Shape — where it wins

3Shape's Dental System is a deep, integrated suite. If your lab lives inside the 3Shape world — Trios scans coming in, Communicate for cases — the workflow is seamless end to end.

  • Integrated ecosystem — scan, design, and case communication in one connected pipeline.
  • Automate — cloud auto-design that scales high-volume, repetitive crown & coping work.
  • Very strong full-arch, implant and splint modules for complex work.
  • Always current — the subscription keeps you on the latest version automatically.

The real difference: own vs. subscribe

Strip away the feature lists and the decision comes down to two questions: do you want to own your software or subscribe to it, and do you want an open toolkit or an integrated one?

Exocad's perpetual model means a larger upfront cost but no bill to keep working — attractive to an independent lab watching fixed costs. 3Shape's subscription spreads cost over time and guarantees you're always current, which suits a lab that wants one coherent, supported pipeline and doesn't want to think about versions.

Neither is “cheaper” in the abstract. Exocad is cheaper to keep; 3Shape is cheaper to start and stay current. The right answer depends on your volume and how long you'll run the software.

Which should your lab pick?

  • Independent lab, mixed hardware, cost-conscious → Exocad usually wins on total cost of ownership.
  • Growing lab standardizing a digital pipeline, high repeat volume → 3Shape's integration and Automate pay off.
  • You only need a few units a week → honestly, neither license is worth it yet — read the next section.
